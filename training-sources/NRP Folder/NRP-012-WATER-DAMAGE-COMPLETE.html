📚 Industry Standards & Classifications

A plain-language summary of how water damage is described. Follow the current requirements in your own IICRC course material and the manufacturer's instructions.

💧 Water Categories

  • Category 1: Cleaner water, needing the least protection
  • Category 2: Contaminated water that can cause illness
  • Category 3: Heavily contaminated water with serious health risks

📊 Damage Classes

  • Class 1: Little absorption
  • Class 2: More absorption into materials
  • Class 3: The most water, often absorbed from above
  • Class 4: Deep saturation in dense materials needing specialty drying

🌡️ Environmental Factors

  • Temperature: Warmth helps water evaporate
  • Humidity: Lower humidity helps materials dry
  • Air Movement: Moving air carries moisture away from surfaces
  • Dehumidification: Removes moisture from the air so drying can continue

⏱️ Drying Timeframes

  • Class 1: Usually the shortest drying time
  • Class 2: A longer drying time
  • Class 3: Longer again
  • Class 4: Usually the longest drying time

📝 Professional Assessment - Water Damage Restoration

Instructions: Answer all questions based on industry standards and Australian practices. Passing score: 80%

Question 1: What should decide when structural drying is complete?

Answer: b) Readings reaching the drying goal set for the job. Drying is complete when measured readings show the materials have reached the goal, which helps prevent secondary damage. Follow the current requirements in your own IICRC course material and the manufacturer's instructions.

Question 2: How should the number and placement of air movers be decided?

Answer: c) From the affected area, the materials and the manufacturer's instructions. Air movers should be placed to keep air moving across wet surfaces. Follow the current requirements in your own IICRC course material and the manufacturer's instructions.

Question 3: Why does temperature matter during structural drying?

Answer: b) Warmer, comfortable conditions help water evaporate. Follow the current requirements in your own IICRC course material and the manufacturer's instructions.
