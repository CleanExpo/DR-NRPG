1. Water Damage Classes & Categories

IICRC S500 Water Categories

Category 1 - Clean Water

Source: Broken supply lines, melting ice/snow, rainwater

Health Risk: Minimal - no substantial risk

Response Time: Becomes Category 2 over time

PPE Required: Standard work gloves, safety glasses

Category 2 - Grey Water

Source: Washing machine overflow, toilet overflow with urine, dishwasher discharge

Health Risk: Moderate - contains significant contamination

Response Time: Becomes Category 3 over time

PPE Required: N95 mask, gloves, protective clothing

Category 3 - Black Water

Source: Sewage, flooding from rivers/streams, stagnant water

Health Risk: High - contains pathogenic agents

Response Time: Immediate containment required

PPE Required: Full PPE including respirator, protective suit, gloves, eye protection

Water Class Evaporation Rate Water Intrusion Drying Time Equipment Needed
Class 1 Slow Rate <5% of area, minimal absorption 3-5 days 1 dehu per 1000-1500 sq ft
Class 2 Fast Rate 5-40% of area, absorption into materials 5-7 days 1 dehu per 500-750 sq ft
Class 3 Fastest Rate >40% of area, ceiling affected 7-10 days 1 dehu per 200-300 sq ft
Class 4 Specialty Drying Deep saturation, bound water 10+ days Specialty equipment required
Australian Standard AS/NZS 3500.2:2021: All water damage restoration must comply with plumbing and drainage standards. Category 3 water requires licensed plumbers for sewage-related issues.

4. Psychrometric Principles

Key Psychrometric Relationships

Property Formula/Relationship Drying Significance Target Range
Specific Humidity Grains/lb (GPP) Actual moisture in air 30-60 GPP optimal
Vapor Pressure kPa = RH × Saturation VP Drying potential >0.5 kPa deficit
Dew Point Temperature at 100% RH Condensation risk >10°C surface temp
Enthalpy Total heat content Energy for evaporation 45-55 kJ/kg
Drying Goal Formula: Target GPP = Current GPP - (Current GPP × 0.4). This creates the vapor pressure deficit needed for effective drying per IICRC S500 standards.
