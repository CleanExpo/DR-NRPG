1. Water Damage Classes & Categories

Water Categories

Category 1 - Clean Water

Source: Broken supply lines, melting ice/snow, rainwater

Health Risk: Minimal - no substantial risk

Response Time: Becomes Category 2 over time

PPE Required: Standard work gloves, safety glasses

Category 2 - Grey Water

Source: Washing machine overflow, toilet overflow with urine, dishwasher discharge

Health Risk: Moderate - contains significant contamination

Response Time: Becomes Category 3 over time

PPE Required: N95 mask, gloves, protective clothing

Category 3 - Black Water

Source: Sewage, flooding from rivers/streams, stagnant water

Health Risk: High - contains pathogenic agents

Response Time: Immediate containment required

PPE Required: Full PPE including respirator, protective suit, gloves, eye protection

Note: Drying times and equipment numbers depend on the job. Follow the current requirements in your own IICRC course material and the manufacturer's instructions.

Water Class Evaporation Rate Water Intrusion Drying Time Equipment Needed
Class 1 Slowest evaporation Small area, little absorption Shortest drying time Lightest equipment load
Class 2 Faster evaporation Larger area, absorption into materials Moderate drying time Moderate equipment load
Class 3 Fastest evaporation Widest wetting, often including walls and ceilings Longer drying time Heavier equipment load
Class 4 Specialty Drying Deep saturation, bound water Longest drying time Specialty equipment and methods
Australian Standard AS/NZS 3500.2:2021: All water damage restoration must comply with plumbing and drainage standards. Category 3 water requires licensed plumbers for sewage-related issues.

4. Psychrometric Principles

Key Psychrometric Relationships

Property Formula/Relationship Drying Significance Practical Use
Specific Humidity Grains/lb (GPP) Actual moisture in air Compare with the goal set for the job
Vapor Pressure kPa = RH × Saturation VP Drying potential A bigger gap between air and material dries faster
Dew Point Temperature at 100% RH Condensation risk Keep surfaces above the dew point to avoid condensation
Enthalpy Total heat content Energy for evaporation Warmer air carries more energy for evaporation
Drying Goal: Set a drying goal for each job so that the air is drier than the wet materials. This difference is what draws moisture out of the materials. Follow the current requirements in your own IICRC course material and the manufacturer's instructions.

Module Assessment

Question 1: Why is dehumidifier capacity matched to the size of the area and the amount of water involved?

  • The same number of units is used on every job
  • So the equipment can remove the moisture that needs to be removed
  • So the equipment costs as little as possible
  • Capacity has no effect on drying

Question 2: What can happen to Category 2 water if it is left for a long time?

  • It turns into clean water
  • Nothing changes
  • Contamination can increase and it can become Category 3
  • It dries out without leaving moisture behind

Question 3: Why does effective drying aim for a gap between the moisture in the air and the moisture in the materials?

  • To make the room feel warmer
  • Drier air draws moisture out of the wet materials
  • To reduce the number of readings needed
  • To avoid using any dehumidification

Question 4: Which situation is most likely to need specialty drying equipment?

  • Light dampness on a small area
  • A small area with limited absorption
  • A wide area of wet walls and ceiling
  • Deep saturation in dense, slow-drying materials

Question 5: How should protective equipment be chosen for a Category 3 (black water) job?

  • Standard gloves only
  • N95 mask and gloves
  • Protective equipment that suits the contamination and health risk, as set out in your course material and site safety plan
  • Safety glasses and boots
